['How does DNA methylation lead to gene repression?', "And a little on intragenic methylation&\n\nOne of the first things you learn about in regards to epigenetics is the negative correlation between methylated DNA promoters and the expression of the relevant gene. In my experience, however, the mechanisms behind this are far more interesting and a little less discussed. \\n \nOne reason for this may be the lack of concrete understanding. There is little we can say with absolute confidence about the foundational biochemistry behind epigenetic repression. \\n \\n \nThe methylation of bases may affect the ability of transcriptional proteins to bind to the gene. One such way this may occur is due to the action of methyl-CpG-binding domain proteins (MCBDs). Methylated (CpG) DNA may bind MCBDs who, in turn, recruit proteins like histone deacetylases and other remodelling proteins. The action of such proteins modify the histones and form inactive chromatin. \nHistone deacetylases remove acetyl groups from N-acetyl lysine on the histone tails. in absence of acetyl groups, the tail becomes more positive in charge and gains in affinity to DNA. The histone ultimately binds chromatin more tightly and it becomes inaccessible to transcription factors and polymerases. \\n \nIt has been suggested (you'll hear that phrase a lot here) that the physical blocking of transcription factors by the additional methyl group may be the primary method of repression. \\n \nIn the case of non-CpG methylation, opinion is split. Some claim that (at least in humans) only CpG methylation is responsible for gene repression, and CpA/CpT/CpA methylation is simply due to hypersensitivity of DNA methyltransferases. Some propose that non-CpG methylation can influence gene expression, but in ways that are not confidently understood.\nOf course, there are more sites suitable for methylation than CpXs, from CpXpGs to CpXpXs. These are found more in plants and probably assist further in repression of genes and transposons:\nMethylation is vital in transposons, as they are repressed regardless of their transposition. Methylation of symmetrical sites (CpXpG is complementary to GpX'pC) is maintained during transcription. \\n \n(;) \n/** Gene Body Methylation /** \nThis is a slight departure from gene repression but we will discuss downregulation of spurious transcription so all is well! \\n \nGene body methylation is a little paradoxical. In genes where the promoter regions are heavily methylated, expression is heavily repressed. However, genes where the gene body is methylated tend to be expressed /* more /* often. \\n \nIt has been suggested that DNA methylation is primarily a mechanism for stopping spurious transcription rather than regulating entire promoters. It was shown at the human SHANK3 locus that gene body methylation correlated negatively with intragenic transcription. If this was the case though, you would expect intragenic DNA methylation to exist mostly at cryptic start sites and intronic promoters - this is not the case. \\n \nMany models have been proposed to explain this. It's been suggested that intragenic methylation and the repression of intragenic transcription is simply a side product of higher DNA accessibility to methylating enzymes. Even more confusingly, methylation of a gene body is /* not /* monotonic with frequency of expression. Genes in the 5th quintile of expression show the same level of methylation as those in the 2nd. The 3rd and 4th quintiles show the highest methylation levels and the 1st the lowest. Gene body methylation is confidently independent of gene length. \\n \n"]
